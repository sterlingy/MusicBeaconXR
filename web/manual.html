Music Beacon XR is a music map: walk a listener between beacons, and the three closest beacons' songs blend into one live track.

Quick start

You hear music within a minute: open the page, press Start, and move the listener.

  1. Open the page. On the PC, run python web/serve.py in the project folder and open http://127.0.0.1:8765. On a phone or tablet, see Phones and tablets below.
  2. Pick a field at the top: Test grid, Globe or Local grid. For Local grid, set its side length and beacon spacing first.
  3. Press ▶ Start the music (or ▶ Start at the top). Headphones are recommended.
  4. Move the listener: drag or tap on the map, or use the arrow keys or WASD. In Local grid, the listener follows your real location instead.
  5. Watch The blend panel: it shows the three beacons you hear and how much of each.

How the music works

Every beacon has its own song, and you always hear the three beacons closest to you, mixed by distance.

  • Closer means louder. The nearest beacon gets the biggest share. Standing on a beacon plays its song alone (100%).
  • No jumps. As a fourth beacon becomes one of the three closest, the one it replaces has already faded to 0%, so the music changes smoothly.
  • One key at a time. The strongest song sets the key, chords and phrase. The key only changes on a bar line, and only when another song clearly leads (by the Key change margin, 8% by default).
  • Tempo glides. The tempo moves toward the average of the three songs, a little each bar.
  • Six instruments. Drums, bass, keys, lead, pluck and texture. Each instrument's level and sound are blended from the three songs; each beat, every part takes its notes from one song, chosen by its share.

The songs are generated from each beacon's ID, so a beacon always plays the same song, on every device, unless someone edits or shares a different one.

Song structure

Every beacon plays a full song, 2 to 5 minutes long, built from sections like a pop song: for example intro, verse, chorus, verse, chorus, bridge, chorus, outro. Some songs add a pre-chorus, a breakdown or a double last chorus.

SectionWhat it does
IntroOpens the song, with anywhere from one instrument to all of them
VerseSteady; later verses usually echo the first with one part swapped
Pre-chorusBuilds tension into the chorus
ChorusThe high point: its own chords, busier drums, a lead hook, brighter sound; the last chorus is the biggest
BridgeContrast: new chords, often half-time drums or none
BreakdownPared right back, sometimes one instrument alone
OutroWinds down; most fade out, some just stop
  • Instruments change by section. Each song uses its own set of 2 to 6 instruments, and each section, every repeat included, picks its own lineup from them. Sometimes an instrument comes in partway through a section.
  • The sound moves with the song. Filters open up in a chorus and close down in quiet sections, the drums thin out or fill in, and a snare fill leads into a bigger section.
  • Songs keep playing while you're away. Each beacon broadcasts its song on a shared clock, so everyone at a beacon hears the same section at the same moment.
  • Where to see it. Now playing shows the leading song's section and bar. Each row of The blend shows its song's section, time (for example 1:42 / 3:36), energy and a strip of its sections with a playhead. The Beacon song panel shows the length, each section's chords, the instruments playing now, and each instrument's preset.

Local grid

The Local grid is a square of beacons centered on you, a kilometer apart by default, and it moves the listener as you move.

Choosing a size. Before you tap Local grid, or any time after, set two things in the top bar. The page remembers both.

  • Local grid, each side: the length of each side of the square, 1, 5, 10, 50 (the default) or 100 km.
  • Beacon every … km: the spacing between beacons, any number from 0.01 km (10 m) to 100 km; decimals are fine. The default is 1 km.

The side is rounded to a whole number of spacings. A side holds at most 150 spacings (22,801 beacons), so a very fine spacing shrinks the grid and the page tells you: a beacon every 10 m gives a 1.5 km grid. With an odd number of spacings per side you stand inside a square of beacons rather than on a center beacon.

Following you. When you tap Local grid, the page asks for your location, centers the grid on you and ticks Follow me. The listener then moves with you.

  • Walking by hand, dragging, or turning on Auto-walk stops following.
  • Center on me (or ticking Follow me again) starts it again.
  • If location isn't available, the grid starts in London; use Jump to… or the lat, lon box to move it.

Re-centering. When you travel 40% of the side from the grid's middle (at least 0.6 of a spacing), the grid re-centers on you. Every grid sits on one worldwide lattice of points 1 m apart, so the beacons around you keep their songs and the music carries on. A jump to somewhere the old grid didn't cover starts fresh.

Same place, same songs. A given street corner has the same beacon and the same song for everyone, at every grid size. Spacings that line up share beacons too: every 2 km beacon is also a 1 km beacon.

Synths page

The Synths page lets you play and shape each of the six instruments on its own; open it with Synths ↗ in the top bar, or click an instrument's name under Instruments to bring over the sound it's playing right now.

InstrumentPartCharacter
PulsarDrumsKick, snare, hats and clap from oscillators and noise
UndertowBassTwo detuned oscillators and a sub through a low-pass filter
LumenKeysChords and pads with two oscillators per note
CometLeadA two-oscillator lead with vibrato and delay
DriftwoodPluckPlucked strings, mallets and bells
TangleTextureFM drones and evolving textures
  • Play. Tap the on-screen keys, or use the computer keys A W S E D F T G Y H U J K (Z and X change octave); Pulsar has drum pads on A S D F G. Play loop runs a 4-bar demo at the Tempo you set; Chord plays a chord per key and Note length sets how long notes ring.
  • Presets. Each instrument has 20 factory presets in the picker under its name; ◀ ▶ step through them. Save as… keeps your own sound under "Yours", and Delete removes one of yours. Any change shows the sound as "(edited)".
  • Animate a control. Press ∿ beside a control, then choose a Shape (sine, triangle, saw up, saw down, square, random steps or drift), how long one sweep takes (Every 1/4 bar to 32 bars) and how far it swings (Depth). An animated control shows its moving value in orange. Filters, resonance, envelopes, levels, FM depth, drum tune and decay, and the effect sends can all be animated.
  • Glide. The bass and lead slide from note to note; Lumen slides each note of a chord into the next chord; Tangle slides its drone. Set it with each instrument's Glide control.
  • Reset, Random, Copy and Paste. Reset returns to the plain default sound; Random starts from a preset and pushes it somewhere new; Copy JSON and Paste JSON move a sound in and out as text.

Your sounds and saved presets are kept in that browser. Generated songs use these same presets, so the sounds you hear on the map are ones you can open and change here.

My beacons

The My beacons panel on the Globe tab lets you add beacons of your own; they stay on your device until you share them. Making a beacon needs your location, accurate to 250 m or better, and works anywhere in the world. Sharing works anywhere too.

  • Each grid and map can be turned on or off (several at once, each in its own tint), gone to, renamed, duplicated, exported, imported as a copy, or deleted. New songs regenerates a whole grid; New song in the Beacon song panel regenerates one beacon.
  • Walk out of your grid and the panel offers the next grid along, lined up with the first.
  • Grids are made where you really are (your location), not where you're looking. A second grid in the same spot needs a different size or spacing; grids that only overlap are fine, and where two beacons share a spot one song plays.
  • How they sound: your beacons blend the three nearest within their range. They play at full strength out to three-quarters of their range, then fade out as the globe fades in. The line under Now playing shows the mix, for example User 70% · Global 30%.
  • Several songs on one spot (up to 20): the Beacon song panel lists them, and Where several songs share a spot, play picks one: random (changes daily, the same for everyone), my favorite (★), mine, original or latest.

Shared map and sharing

Beacons other people shared play alongside yours, read around you as you travel and saved on your device so they still play offline. They count as "User" in the mix, draw in grey, and can't be edited; Shared map in My beacons shows what's nearby and turns them off.

To share your own, sign in under Account (for sharing) at the bottom of My beacons. There's no password: you type your email and either tap the link in the email (website) or type its 6-digit code (website and app). Listening never needs an account.

  • Share on a grid or map, Share all on planted songs, or Share this song on one planted song puts it on the shared map; Unshare takes it off.
  • Before your first share you accept the terms once: shared songs are CC BY 4.0, a shared beacon shows a place you've been (dated to the day, never with your name), and you can unshare or delete everything at any time.
  • Limits: 5 grids, 20 songs and 20 planted songs a day per person.
  • Report a shared beacon from the Beacon song panel (offensive, spam, unsafe place, private place, other); after reports from 3 people it hides until it's reviewed.
  • Favorites: your ★ on a shared beacon counts toward the number of people who favorited it.
  • Shared maps near you lists public maps around you to add as your own copy.
  • Delete my account… removes your account and everything you shared, favorited and reported; your beacons stay on your device, unshared.

Following a flight

Type a tail number (like N733YX), a flight number as printed on your ticket (like DL934) or as planes broadcast it (like DAL934) or a transponder code beside ✈ Follow flight on the Globe tab: a real aircraft's live position then moves you, so you hear the beacons it flies over.

  • A plane points along its track, up in the air, and the line under the map shows its registration, flight, aircraft type, altitude and speed. Once its route is known and confirmed, a green arc runs from its origin to its destination with the plane riding just above it, and a faint line drops to where you are.
  • Positions update every 20 seconds; in between, the plane glides along its speed and heading, so the music never jumps.
  • Walking by hand, going anywhere yourself (Jump, Move, My location, a city, typed coordinates, Go on your beacons, Walk here, Auto-walk) or ■ Stop flight ends it. Press ● Record walk while following to keep the flight's soundtrack.
  • The plane has to be in the air and sending its position. On the ground or out of receiver coverage, it isn't found.
  • Flight data comes from adsb.lol (ODbL). Its free service allows a few hours of following a day in total, so "busy" means try again later.

Phones and tablets

The easiest way on a phone is the public site, sterlingy.github.io/MusicBeaconXR: it's secure (HTTPS), so location works anywhere with no PC running. Or install the app (see The app). The PC server below is for trying changes before they're published.

Phones only share location with secure (HTTPS) pages, so run the PC server in HTTPS mode and open it from the phone over home Wi-Fi or ZeroTier.

On the PC

  1. In the project folder, run python web/serve.py --https.
  2. It prints the addresses to open, for example https://10.0.0.12:8443 (home network) and https://10.241.70.124:8443 (ZeroTier). Leave it running.

On the phone

  1. Join the same network: home Wi-Fi, or the ZeroTier app with shivoo_vpn switched on.
  2. Open the https address in the browser. Type the https:// part; without it the browser may try plain http and fail.
  3. At the certificate warning, tap Advanced, then Proceed (Chrome) or Show Details → visit this website (Safari). The page uses its own certificate, so this warning is expected, once per device.
  4. Set the Local grid's side length and spacing, tap Local grid, and allow location when asked.
  5. Tap ▶ Start the music.

Pixel and other Android phones need three things set once:

  • ZeroTier: in its Settings, turn on Allow mobile data, and have the phone authorized on the network at my.zerotier.com.
  • Chrome: Settings → Apps → Chrome → Permissions → Nearby devices → Allow. Without it Chrome can't reach the PC and shows ERR_TOO_MANY_RETRIES.
  • Turn off any other VPN, such as VPN by Google, while you use ZeroTier: Android runs one VPN at a time.

Screen and music. Phones pause web music when the screen turns off, so while music plays the page keeps the screen on. If the music stops anyway (a call, another app, locking the phone), it starts again when you come back to the page. Following your location also needs the page on screen.

Away from home. The phone can only reach the PC on the home network or ZeroTier. Elsewhere, the page would need to be hosted on a public HTTPS address.

Most problems are a missing permission or the phone not reaching the PC; the PC server's window logs every phone visit and location attempt (never your coordinates), which shows which one it is.
